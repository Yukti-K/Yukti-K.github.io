Research / Mission

Making sense of healthcare complexity.

How can we learn from complex, incomplete, and evolving healthcare data to make better decisions about patient risk, care pathways, and limited resources?

My research brings together operations research, statistical modeling, and applied analytics to study these questions. I develop and apply quantitative approaches to identify patterns that are difficult to see, understand how risks change, and inform decisions about where care and resources may be needed most.

01 / Research directions

Three questions, one challenge

Healthcare decisions depend on understanding who faces risk, when it changes, and how and where limited resources can be directed. My selected projects examine these connected challenges through different quantitative approaches.

WHO · Identifying risk

Dynamic Cardiovascular Risk Stratification in Type 2 Diabetes Using Bipartite Stochastic Block Models for Specialty Referral Decisions

This work focuses on which patients face elevated cardiovascular risk and how dynamic stratification could inform specialty referral decisions.

Approach: Bipartite stochastic block models · Risk stratification · Specialty referral decisions

WHEN · Understanding timing

Order-Based Bayesian Network Modeling of Early Detection and Post-Diagnosis Control for Cardiovascular Disease Risk in Type 2 Diabetes

A preprint using EHR data from 9,450 primary-care patients to study relationships between early diabetes detection, post-diagnosis glycemic control, and subsequent cardiovascular risk.

Approach: Order-based Bayesian network · Machine Learning

HOW & WHERE · Allocating resources

Multi-Criteria Clinic Allocation in Massachusetts to Reduce Access to Healthcare Disparities

Research on clinic allocation in Western Massachusetts, examining how rurality and transportation barriers can inform equitable healthcare facility placement to address disparities in access. Published in the proceedings of the IISE Annual Conference & Expo (2024).

Approach: Mixed Integer Programming · Rurality Index

02 / Emerging directions

New frontiers in healthcare research

I’m interested in research that moves beyond prediction to understand causes, compare interventions, and support better decisions in healthcare. Several directions especially intrigue me—both as methodological challenges and as opportunities to tackle important health problems.

Decisions under uncertainty

Optimization, AI & adaptive care

Developing reliable decision tools that combine prediction with optimization, sequential decisions, evolving patient information, and operational constraints.

Evidence for interventions

Causal inference & precision medicine

Understanding heterogeneous treatment effects through longitudinal and multimodal data, including EHR trajectories, molecular measurements, and patient-generated data.

Value, outcomes & policy

Health economics & decision modeling

Exploring cost-effectiveness, disease and policy simulation, population health, budget impact, and how evidence informs clinical and health-system choices.

Problems and places for impact

Understudied needs & healthcare innovation

I'm particularly drawn to women's health, diseases disproportionately affecting women, and rare diseases, alongside opportunities at the intersection of biotechnology, pharmaceutical research, healthcare delivery, and entrepreneurship.

These interests reflect questions and research possibilities I want to investigate, not a fixed list of future specialties.
